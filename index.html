UNT. JIRI PROCHAZKA
Logline

At the peak of his career, UFC fighter Jiří Procházka sets out to reclaim his title — but a devastating loss just days before the birth of his first child forces him to confront who he is beyond the identity he has built as a fighter.

The Story

We meet Jiří Procházka just weeks before his legacy-defining fight to reclaim the UFC light heavyweight championship, and days before the birth of his first child.

One of the fastest-rising champions in UFC history, Procházka won the light heavyweight championship in 2022 in just three fights, building a reputation for his unpredictable style and near-monastic commitment to a samurai code. A national symbol in the Czech Republic, he moves between the quiet simplicity of his small Central European village and the global spotlight of sold-out arenas.

What begins as a championship comeback story evolves into a deeply personal meditation on resilience, discipline, and a relentless pursuit to test one's limits.

Act I — The Origin

Jiří Procházka's story begins in a small Czech village, where the loss of his father at a young age forced him into the role of head of the family. Searching for direction, he found The Book of Five Rings, a classic Japanese warrior text and philosophy that would become the foundation of how he lives, trains, and fights today.

With this inspiration, Jiří set out to build himself from the ground up. In 2015, he joined Japan's Rizin league, living away from home for the first time and developing a style that was unpredictable and relentless. It's also where his philosophy started to take new form, building on The Book of Five Rings and turning it into a code that transcended fighting.

When he joins UFC in 2020, his rise is immediate. In just three fights, he captures the light heavyweight title, becoming one of the fastest champions in the organization's history. His philosophy, unconventional training, and willingness to push himself to extremes begin to build a kind of mystique — he quickly becomes a fan favorite.

In 2022, at the height of this ascent, a serious injury forces him to vacate the belt, putting everything on hold for a year.

Now he's preparing to come back and fight for the title again, with his first child on the way. For the first time, he has to consider how this all fits into the life he's building outside of his career.

Act II — The Loss

Act II stays close to Jiří in the weeks leading up to the fight and everything that follows. He and his team are confident heading in. In Round 1, Jiří severely injures his opponent and has all but won the fight. Then something in his demeanor changes — in a moment, Jiří is on the ground, knocked out cold.

The global crowd goes silent in what will go down as one of the most shocking title fight losses in history. We stay with Jiří through the aftermath with his team: the flight home to Prague with a broken jaw, his post-fight surgery, and the birth of his daughter, all within a matter of days.

After the loss, the shift in his fandom is immediate. Someone who has been beloved for years for his unpredictability is suddenly paying the price for it with an unexpected wave of backlash.

At his lowest point mentally and emotionally — his first child is born.

Act III — The Rebuild

The birth of Jiří's daughter, Elenka, changes the meaning of the comeback. After a career built on discipline and self-actualization, we see Jiří step into fatherhood and relinquish his freedom.

Coming off a devastating loss and public backlash, he begins to rebuild — restructuring his team, questioning old methods, and letting go of what no longer serves him.

We follow him through the next stage of his transformation: a climb up Mount Fuji, a new training camp in Thailand, and the road toward his next fight at Madison Square Garden.

The question we explore with Jiří is no longer just whether he can win again. It is whether he can change — as a fighter, as a father, and as a man — without losing the code that made him who he is.

Character Overview
Jiří Procházka.

Jiří's story reflects a shifting idea of what strength and identity can look like. He fights to test himself. Where most elite athletes are driven by control, strategy, and legacy, Jiří openly invites chaos. He allows himself to be hit, to be pushed to the edge, because for him the fight is as internal as it is physical.

This comes from a longer search for something more personal — whether that's structure, meaning, or a code to live by. This makes him both unpredictable and palpably present.

He brings a straightforwardness and sincerity to this violent and unforgiving environment that feels refreshing and genuine. Whether it's shaving his head in solidarity with a fan battling cancer or speaking openly about his personal philosophy, his actions feel instinctive and from the heart.

For many in the Czech Republic, he represents something closer to an Olympian figure — a symbol of discipline, resilience, and pride. He has embraced that responsibility without turning it into spectacle.

Why This Film — Why Now
Behind the myth,a man.

Masculinity, and most recently the "manosphere," have been at the forefront of conversation for more than a decade. In an era of shifting identity, young men are looking not to those who foster a good life, but to those who garner the most attention — Andrew Tate, Clavicular, Sneako.

UFC has one of the youngest and most male-skewing audiences in major sports, with more than 70% of its fanbase males 18–49, and 40% of them in the 18–34 group. UFC is viewed by many as a brutal expression of masculinity — violent, aggressive, primal. Combat sports stars in the modern era tend to be bombastic and arrogant, with their antics outside the ring often overshadowing their accomplishments (Conor McGregor, anyone?).

Jiří is the counterpoint to all of this.

He does not view winning as fuel for his ego, but as a reflection of his honor and discipline. He treats those around him with kindness, mercy, and respect — even to the point of losing. He lives by a code of honor that seems to have disappeared from modern Western society, and yet he is still a champion, still a star, and still a fan favorite.

This is not just a comeback story. It's an intimate portrait of a man navigating identity, purpose, and legacy at the highest level of combat sports, just as the foundation of his life abruptly changes course. Behind the myth is a man being forced to question what strength means when winning is no longer enough. In this moment of reckoning, a side of Jiří emerges that few have ever seen — not even those closest to him.

Appeal

UFC draws an estimated 700 million fans globally and reaches over 1 billion households worldwide across more than 175 countries and territories. Roughly 43% of its audience falls between the ages of 18 and 34.

It is one of the most followed sports leagues globally, with well over 200 million followers across its social media platforms and billions of organic video views on platforms like YouTube.

Jiří Procházka has been voted the most popular fighter in the UFC. He most recently fought for the light heavyweight championship — an event that drew roughly 2.65M on CBS, with global streams via Paramount+ averaging a record-breaking 4.96M views per minute.

On a narrative level, we're following an individual who channeled his own disadvantage into something productive, building himself through discipline, ambition, and belief. At a time when a lot of young men feel lost or isolated, Jiří's path can serve as an example.
