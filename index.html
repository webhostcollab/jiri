UNT. JIRI PROCHAZKA
Logline

At the peak of his career, UFC fighter Jiří Procházka sets out to reclaim his title — but a devastating loss just days before the birth of his first child forces him to confront who he is beyond the identity he has built as a fighter.

The Story

We met Jiří Procházka just weeks before his fight to reclaim the UFC light heavyweight championship — just days before the birth of his first child.

One of the fastest-rising champions in UFC history, Procházka won the title in 2022 in just three fights. A national symbol in the Czech Republic, he moves between two worlds: a quiet life in a small Central European village, and the global stage of sold-out arenas.

What begins as a story about a championship comeback expands into a meditation on identity, parenthood, and what remains of a man when the version of himself he's built starts to fail.

Act I — The Origin

Jiří's story begins in a small Czech village. After losing his father young, he found direction in The Book of Five Rings — a samurai text that became the foundation of how he lives, trains, and fights.

In 2019 he fought in Japan's Rizin league, where his philosophy took form. He turned an ancient code into something he actually lived by.

Joining UFC in 2020, his rise was immediate — three fights, one title. One of the fastest champions in the organization's history.

Then, at the height of his ascent, a serious injury forces him out for a year. His first child is on the way. Everything he's built is about to be tested.

Act II — The Loss

Act II follows Jiri through the weeks leading up to his fight against Carlos Ulberg — and everything that follows the loss. In round one, Jiri nearly finishes it. Then he gets knocked out cold.

The flight home to Prague with a broken jaw. Surgery. An unexpected wave of public backlash from someone who has only ever been widely loved.

We stay with him through all of it — the disorientation, the grief, the silence.

At his lowest point mentally and emotionally — his first child is born.

Act III — The Rebuild

The birth of Jiri's daughter Elenka reframes everything. There's something immediate and external that now carries weight — and with it, a version of freedom is lost.

He restructures his team, lets go of what isn't working, and faces the backlash from his loss. Finding strength not in winning, but in changing course.

Climb Mount Fuji. Training camp in Thailand. The road back to MSG — and the light heavyweight championship.

Character Overview
Jiří Procházka。

Jiří fights to test himself. Where most elite athletes are driven by control, strategy, and legacy, Jiří openly invites chaos. He allows himself to be hit, to be pushed to the edge, because for him the fight is as internal as it is physical.

This comes from a longer search for something more personal — whether that's structure, meaning, or a code to live by. This makes him both unpredictable and palpably present.

He doesn't lean into trash talk or performance. He brings a straightforwardness and sincerity to this violent, unforgiving environment — his actions feel instinctive and from the heart.

For many in the Czech Republic, he represents something closer to an Olympian figure — a symbol of discipline, resilience, and pride.

Why This Film — Why Now
Behind the myth,
a man.
This is not just a comeback story. It's an intimate portrait of a man navigating identity, purpose, and legacy at the highest level of combat sports — just as the foundation of his life abruptly changes course.

Masculinity has been at the forefront of conversation for more than a decade. In an era of shifting identity, young men are looking not to those who foster a good life, but those who garner the most attention.

UFC has one of the youngest and most male-skewing audiences in major sports — more than 70% of its fanbase are males 18–49. Combat sports stars of the modern era tend to be bombastic and arrogant. Jiri is the counterpoint to all of this.

He does not view winning as fuel for his ego, but a reflection of his honor and discipline. He lives by a code that seems to have disappeared from modern western society — and yet is still a champion, still a star, and still a fan favorite.
